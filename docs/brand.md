# Lighthouse identity

Lighthouse is an engineering baseline for secure, efficient software development with agents in the loop. Its identity should be direct and recognizable: a real lighthouse, drawn with the clarity of a software product.

## Position

**A clear path to engineering excellence.**

*Proven techniques and best practices for a practical, AI-driven SDLC.*

Lighthouse helps teams make engineering expectations adoptable and verifiable. It gives people and agents a steady reference point, with explicit scope, evidence, and human ownership where decisions matter.

## Visual idea

The core mark is a tall, blue and white lighthouse with a visible lantern and a focused beam. Its clean geometry keeps the silhouette legible at small sizes; the beam gives it direction and energy. Use subtle grid lines or horizon strokes in large compositions to connect the maritime symbol to engineering. Keep the lighthouse itself simple and literal.

## Color

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Night ink | `#0D0F17` | Background and dark surfaces |
| Signal blue | `#4DA3FF` | Tower, beam, and active accents |
| Beacon blue | `#8FC2FF` | Light, edges, and secondary emphasis |
| Deep blue | `#1F6FD1` | Tower shade and structural detail |
| White | `#F8FAFF` | Lighthouse face and high contrast text |
| Cool gray | `#8A96AD` | Quiet labels and supporting detail |

Keep blue and white as the main identity. Reserve brighter blue for the signal and use cool gray for secondary information. These brand colors belong to Lighthouse assets; the optional interface theme has its own semantic tokens and palette.

## Type and layout

Use a sturdy modern sans serif for names and headings, paired with a readable system sans serif for body copy. Use monospace only for commands, versions, and short technical labels. Prefer generous space, crisp alignment, and strong contrast.

## Voice

Lighthouse sounds like a pragmatic staff engineer: concise, specific, quietly confident, and comfortable with technical language. Its geekiness comes through precision and useful detail. State the evidence behind a claim and distinguish advice from a configured check.

For unattended work, explain scope, limits, stop conditions, evidence, and who owns acceptance. Do not imply that automation can authorize its own expansion, merge, or release.

### Examples

- **Do:** “Run `lighthouse verify` to check the manifest and generated guidance for drift.”
- **Do:** “The loop can repair this twice. If verification still fails, it stops and leaves the evidence for review.”
- **Do:** “This is recommended guidance; the repository has not wired it into a required CI check.”
- **Avoid:** “Your autonomous engineering command center.”
- **Avoid:** “Zero-risk, fully autonomous delivery.”
- **Avoid:** “It just works.”
